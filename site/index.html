How WAR is calculated

  1. Start percentage. ESPN publishes the share of its leagues starting each player. Seasons up to 2026 have one frozen value per player; from 2027 the value is captured every week.
  2. Consensus lineup. Each week the most-started players with a game fill every position's starting slots for 12 teams, then the most-started leftover RB, WR and TE fill the flex.
  3. Slot means and spread. Over the season, each slot's average points and variance from those lineups. The opponent is a normal distribution: mean = sum of slot means, spread = square root of the summed variances.
  4. Replacement cut. The three most-started players past each position's cut are that week's replacement players; their season average is the replacement level.
  5. Weekly win chance. A player's week is their actual points in a lineup whose other slots sit at the slot means, so the win chance is the share of the opponent distribution below the league mean plus (score − slot mean). A week with no game played takes the replacement level; a game played for zero counts as zero.
  6. Expected wins and WAR. Win chances sum over regular-season weeks 1 to 14. WAR subtracts what a replacement-level player would earn every week, so a replacement season is exactly 0.
