How good are these odds?

Past seasons replayed through the same simulation, compared with what happened

What was checked

Every completed season was replayed week by week with the schedule, rosters, projections and scoring it really had, using score noise learned from the other seasons only. The chances the site would have shown after each week are then compared with the real result. If the odds are honest, teams shown at 30% should reach the outcome about 30% of the time.

    Predicted vs observed

    Each dot is one 10-point band of predicted chance; its size is the number of cases in the band. Dots on the diagonal are perfectly calibrated; above it the odds were too cautious, below it too confident. Hover a dot for the numbers.

    Brier score: mean squared error of the chances (0 is perfect, 0.25 is a coin flip on a 50/50 outcome). The baseline reads the standings: base rate before the season, then each team's record extrapolated over the remaining weeks. Skill is how much better the simulation is than that baseline (1 = perfect, 0 = no better).

    Is the score noise the right width?

    Left: where each real score landed within its simulated range, in tenths. Right noise gives a flat chart; piles at the ends mean the simulation is too narrow, a hump in the middle means too wide. Right: how often the real score fell inside the simulated 50%, 80% and 95% ranges, which should match the nominal figure. "Simulated lineup" scores the players the simulation started rather than the lineup the manager actually started.

    Head-to-head games

    Lineup gap

    The simulation starts each team's best projected lineup. Real managers do not always. The gap is what the best projected lineup would have added, in projected points and in the points those players actually scored.
