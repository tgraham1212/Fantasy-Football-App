CALCULATIONS
============

Every formula and calculation the project performs, one section each. A change that alters a
calculation (its formula, inputs, assumptions or parameters) updates its section here in the
same change; a new calculation module gets a section and is added to the module list in
tests/test_calculations_doc.py.

Each section has the same headings:
  Code:                      the module(s) that perform it
  Data sources:              where its inputs come from and are stored
  Depends on:                other sections it uses
  Formula:                   the calculation in plain-text notation
  In plain English:          how it works, without the maths
  Assumptions and decisions: what it assumes and why it was built this way

Notation: mean(x) is the average, sd(x) the standard deviation, Phi(z) the standard normal
cumulative distribution (the share of a normal curve below z), N(m, s) a normal distribution
with mean m and spread s.

Sections

  2. Player stats and projections
  3. Out statuses
  4. Noise model
  5. Best lineup and the replacement floor

================================================================
1. FANTASY POINTS
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/scoring.py, src/ffapp/stats.py, src/ffapp/league.py
Data sources:  Each league's scoring_settings (data/leagues/<league>/<season>/league.json),
               optional manual overrides in config/leagues.yaml.
Depends on:    2 (the stat lines being scored)
Formula:
  points = sum over stat keys k of  stat[k] * scoring[k]
  Keys that exist on only one side contribute nothing. Sleeper's precomputed totals
  (pts_ppr, pts_half_ppr, pts_std) are skipped.
In plain English:
  Every stat line, projected or actual, is turned into fantasy points by multiplying each
  stat by what the league pays for it and adding them up. The same function scores
  projections and real games, so both leagues are always scored by their own rules.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Sleeper's ready-made point totals are ignored, because they assume standard scoring,
    not the league's settings. Defense bucket keys such as pts_allow_14_20 are real
    scoring categories and are kept.
  - Settings are resolved per week (league.resolve_settings), so a league that changed its
    scoring between seasons is scored correctly for each season.

2. PLAYER STATS AND PROJECTIONS
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/projections.py, src/ffapp/stats.py
Data sources:  Sleeper's weekly projections (data/projections/<season>/week_NN.json, by
               Rotowire via Sleeper) and weekly actual stat lines for every player, free
               agents included (data/stats/<season>/week_NN.json).
Depends on:    none
Formula:
  No arithmetic; records are stored as raw stat lines and scored by section 1.
  A player "played" a week when his stat row carries a computed points total.
In plain English:
  Projections are Sleeper's expectations before a week; stats are what happened. Both are
  stored unscored, so one copy serves both leagues. Played weeks are kept forever and only
  replaced when Sleeper publishes a corrected version.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Projections are the model's only view of a player before kickoff. Calibration notes
    that Sleeper serves only the final version of past projections, which may include
    game-day updates, so replayed forecasts are slightly sharper than live ones were.

3. OUT STATUSES
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/injuries.py
Data sources:  Sleeper player statuses saved weekly (data/injuries/<season>/week_NN.json),
               the stored NFL schedule (data/nfl/<season>/week_NN.json).
Depends on:    2
Formula:
  out = status in {Out, Doubtful, IR, PUP, Sus, NA, DNR}
  labels: Out, Doubtful, IR, PUP, Suspended, Not active, Did not report
  not playing (Games cards): bye, else an out status, else (weeks before statuses were
  saved) inferred out = on the roster, projection 0 or none, and his team played.
In plain English:
  One shared list says who is not expected to play. It drives the Games cards'
  "Not playing" list, the red cross on the Positions page, and the ownership fallback in
  Player WAR (section 16).
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Doubtful counts as out because such players almost never play; Questionable does not.
  - Future weeks use the latest saved statuses, since today's news is the best guess.
  - The simulation itself still reads projections, so a player listed Out is benched only
    once his projection drops (a known limitation).

4. NOISE MODEL
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/noise.py
Data sources:  Every rostered player's projection and actual points in every completed week
               of every stored league-season (both leagues).
Depends on:    1, 2
Formula:
  residual = actual points - projected points
  pools: by position and projected-points band, band edges 0, 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, 18, 22
  pool used = the player's (position, band) pool if it has at least 200 residuals, else
              the whole position's pool
  simulated score = projection + residual drawn at random from that pool
                    (x residual_scale, which is 1.0 unless configured)
In plain English:
  Real scores miss projections. Each simulated score adds a miss drawn from how players at
  the same position and projection level actually missed in past seasons, so a star's
  outcome spreads differently from a backup's, and big upside surprises happen as often as
  they really do.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Empirical pools rather than a fitted distribution keep the real skew (busts and booms).
  - Pools mix both leagues and all seasons; residuals are treated as stationary.
  - In calibration replays, a season's pools come only from the other seasons.
  - residual_scale stays 1.0; calibration (section 18) is the only evidence allowed to
    change it, and only when coverage is off across all seasons and both leagues.

5. BEST LINEUP AND THE REPLACEMENT FLOOR
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/lineup.py
Data sources:  Current rosters (minus IR and taxi), the league's roster slots, projections,
               the week's projected replacement level (section 14).
Depends on:    1, 14
Formula:
  lineup = the legal assignment of rostered players to slots that maximises total projected
           points (FLEX takes RB/WR/TE)
  each slot's value = max(assigned player's projection, projected replacement level for
                          the slot's position)   [FLEX: best of RB, WR, TE levels]
  a slot filled at replacement level draws its noise like a player at that projection
In plain English:
  Each simulated team starts its best projected lineup. A hole from a bye, an injury or a
  thin bench is never worth zero: it is worth what the manager could pick up off waivers.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Managers are assumed to start their best projected lineup; calibration measures the gap
    to what they really started (the lineup gap, section 18).
  - Without the floor, a redraft team's projected strength two weeks out predicted its true
    strength with a slope of 0.50; with it, 0.89 to 1.0 (section 6).

6. ROSTER DRIFT
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/drift.py, src/ffapp/simulate.py
Data sources:  Stored seasons: each team's replacement-floored projected strength under the
               roster it held at week w, scored with week-t projections, against its true
               strength at week t. Results in data/model/<league>.json and in every
               forecast file under "model".
Depends on:    5
Formula:
  For each horizon k = t - w (weeks ahead), a least-squares fit across teams and weeks:
    true_strength - league_mean = slope_k * (projected_strength - league_mean) + e,
    sd_k = sd(e)
  In the simulation, at k weeks ahead, each team's strength is shifted by
    (slope_k - 1) * (strength - league_mean)
  plus a per-run random walk whose step between k-1 and k is
    sqrt(max(0, sd_k^2 - sd_(k-1)^2))
  so the drift's total spread at horizon k is sd_k.
In plain English:
  Rosters change, so a team's edge today only partly survives. From past seasons the model
  knows how much of an edge usually remains some weeks out, and how much random roster
  change piles up. Far-off weeks are therefore less certain than next week.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Estimated per league, because redraft rosters churn far more than dynasty ones.
  - Drift parameters are estimated leave-season-out in calibration replays.
  - The random walk persists through a run, so a team that gets better stays better.

7. SEASON SIMULATION AND STANDINGS
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/simulate.py, src/ffapp/odds.py, src/ffapp/standings.py
Data sources:  The league's matchups and schedule (data/leagues/<league>/<season>/),
               rosters, projections; runs = 10,000 per forecast.
Depends on:    4, 5, 6, 8
Formula:
  as-of week = the last week Sleeper has fully scored (last_scored_leg)
  for each run and each remaining regular-season week:
    team score = sum of its lineup's simulated slot scores (sections 4, 5) + drift (6)
    the higher score wins the scheduled matchup
  standings: wins (a tie counts half), then points scored
  playoff seeds: the league's playoff team count from the standings
In plain English:
  The rest of the season is played 10,000 times on the real schedule. Each run produces a
  final table, a bracket and a champion; counting runs gives the forecasts.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - A week with Thursday's game played but not yet scored counts as unplayed.
  - Backfilled forecasts use the roster each team carried into the following week.
  - bracket rules and standings are verified against every completed season
    (python -m ffapp verify-brackets).

8. PLAYOFF BRACKET
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/bracket.py
Data sources:  Each league's Sleeper settings (playoff_seed_type, playoff_type, playoff
               teams), the simulated standings.
Depends on:    7
Formula:
  Six-team bracket: round 1 seed 4 v 5 and seed 3 v 6, seeds 1 and 2 on byes;
  round 2 seed 1 and seed 2 meet the round-1 winners (re-seeded when playoff_seed_type = 1:
  seed 1 meets the lowest surviving seed; fixed otherwise); final and third place.
  Losers bracket: seeds 7 to 12 with the higher scorer advancing (consolation), or 12 to 7
  with the lower scorer advancing (toilet bowl).
In plain English:
  The playoffs and the losers bracket are played exactly the way each league's Sleeper
  settings play them, inside every simulated run.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Written to run on arrays so thousands of brackets are played at once.

9. FORECAST OUTCOMES
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/simulate.py, src/ffapp/odds.py
Data sources:  The simulation runs (section 7); stored per as-of week in
               data/odds/<league>/<season>/asof_NN.json.
Depends on:    7, 8
Formula:
  chance of an outcome = runs in which the team reaches it / total runs
  outcomes: make the playoffs, first-round bye, reach the final, win the title, losers
  bracket, finish last, avoid the bottom two ("Avoid Loser's Bye")
  projected record = mean simulated wins and losses
In plain English:
  The percentages on the Forecasts table are the share of the 10,000 simulated seasons in
  which each team got there.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - The table's first chance column depends on the league: a toilet-bowl league shows
    "Avoid Loser's Bye", a dynasty league with a consolation bracket shows "#1 Pick".
  - Live weeks write a separate live file so the history never sees partial weeks.

10. GAME FORECASTS
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/forecast.py, src/ffapp/games.py, src/ffapp/slates.py
Data sources:  The simulation runs; the game log of projections frozen at kickoff
               (data/games/<league>/<season>/week_NN.json); slate forecasts
               (data/odds/<league>/<season>/slates_NN.json).
Depends on:    4, 5, 6, 7
Formula:
  per game, from the runs: mean score per team; win probability = runs won / runs
  (a tie counts half for each team); a shared 2-point histogram of both teams' scores
  forecast of record (final weeks): the starters each manager used, each at his last
  logged projection before kickoff (else his stored projection, empty slots 0), simulated
  10,000 times with one week of drift and a seed fixed per league, season and week
  projected playoff games: teams seeded by expected seed; the team with the higher win
  probability advances (in a toilet bowl, the one more likely to score lower)
  game log: one entry per game of the current week per cold run (every 15 minutes in season),
  appended only when a lineup, projection, kickoff state or win probability changed
  slate forecast: the live forecast stored by the first simulating cold run after every game of
  a slate (games sharing a kickoff time) is final, if it kicked off within the last 8 hours
In plain English:
  Every game gets its own forecast from the same simulation. Once a week is over, each
  game also gets a fixed "forecast of record" to compare with what happened.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - The record uses the managers' real starters, not the forecast before the first kickoff
    (owner's choice). Weeks before the log existed use the forecast made the week before.
  - A pairing only counts as real once the week before it is final, because Sleeper fills
    its bracket from the live standings all season.
  - Only the current week is logged: a later week is logged from when it becomes current,
    which is days before its first kickoff, so its kickoff-frozen projections are unaffected.
    With cold runs every 15 minutes, a player's frozen projection is at most 15 minutes older
    than his kickoff (it was up to 3 hours with the old hourly check).
  - The live forecast between cold runs comes from the hot runs (docs/refresh-modes.md) and is
    not logged; cold runs during games skip the simulation, so the log's win probability then
    is the latest cold forecast, and the slate-final run simulates afresh.

11. LINEUP VIEWS AND LINEUP CHOICES
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/views.py
Data sources:  The game log, stored projections, the week's realised replacement level.
Depends on:    5, 10, 14
Formula:
  Model view: each team's best legal lineup that week at pre-kickoff projections, with the
  replacement floor. Actual view: the starters the manager used at the same projections.
  Both simulated with the record's model and no results.
  lineup choices = actual starters' points - model lineup players' points
                   (a replacement fill counts the week's realised replacement level, or the
                   projected level when none is stored)
In plain English:
  The Model | Actual toggle shows what the best lineup would have been against what the
  manager started. "Lineup choices" says how many points the manager's choices gained or
  cost against the model's picks.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - During the live week, only players whose games have kicked off count; fills count once
    the week is final.
  - 2026 weeks 1 to 10 predate the log, so their actual view is rebuilt from stored
    projections and labelled "rebuilt".

12. LIVE FORECASTS
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/live.py, src/ffapp/simulate.py
Data sources:  Sleeper's lineups and per-player points for the week, the stored schedule
               with each game's quarter and clock.
Depends on:    4, 5, 7
Formula:
  share = the share of a player's 60-minute game still to play (1 before kickoff, 0 when
          final, in overtime or without a clock)
  bias  = the mean miss of the residual pool he draws from
  locked starter's score = fixed + share * (projected + bias) + sqrt(share) * (residual - bias)
  where fixed = his points so far
In plain English:
  During a week, a player who has started scores his points so far plus his projection for
  the time left, with the uncertainty shrinking as his game runs out. Open slots keep the
  model's best pick from players who have not kicked off.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - The projection and its bias scale with time left, the scatter with its square root
    (about 71% of a full game's spread at half time). Chosen over Sleeper's live projection
    by the owner.
  - Injuries and benchings during a game are not visible until it is final.

13. WIN PROBABILITY ADDED
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/games.py
Data sources:  The game's pre-kickoff simulation (the forecast of record, or a provisional
               one during the live week), each starter's projection and score, the noise
               pools for his own spread.
Depends on:    4, 10, 12
Formula:
  margin, spread = mean and sd of the simulated score margin (team - opponent)
  before = Phi(margin / spread)
  after  = Phi((margin + score - projected) / after_spread)
  after_spread = sqrt(spread^2 - player_sd^2 * (1 - share))
  WPA = after - before
  A player in progress uses score = so far + share * projection and keeps share of his
  variance.
In plain English:
  How much a player's score moved his team's chance of winning, compared with his
  projection, with everyone else still at their projections.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - The values show the size of each swing and do not add up to the team's total change.
  - Weeks before the log existed use the pre-week forecast's margin and stored projections.

14. REPLACEMENT LEVEL
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/replacement.py
Data sources:  Projections and stats; free agents = projected players on no roster in that
               league that week (stored matchup rosters, or current rosters including IR
               and taxi). Stored in data/replacement/<league>/<season>.json.
Depends on:    1, 2
Formula:
  replacement players (league, week, position) = the N free agents with the highest
  projection that week (N = simulation.replacement_top_n, 5 in this repo's config)
  projected level = mean(their projections)
  realised level  = mean(their actual points)
  season value = (n * mean(realised levels so far) + k * prior) / (n + k)
    k = simulation.prior_weeks (4)
    prior = 0.5 * last season + 0.3 * the season before + 0.2 * the one before that
            (seasons with the same scoring and roster settings; the first stored season
            uses its own mean projected level)
In plain English:
  What a waiver-wire pickup is worth at each position. The simulation uses the projected
  level as the floor for every lineup slot (section 5).
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Players are chosen by projection and scored afterwards, the way a manager picks them
    up; choosing the top scorers after the fact would overstate a pickup's value.
  - N = 5 was chosen from the roster-drift diagnostic in calibration.
  - This free-agent level is not the Player WAR replacement level (section 16), which
    measures fringe starters league-wide.

15. MANAGER RATINGS
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/ratings.py
Data sources:  Every scored regular-season week of every stored season; owners from the
               stored rosters (a vacant roster reads as its last earlier owner, else the
               first later one). Stored in data/ratings/<league>.json.
Depends on:    1
Formula:
  each week: all teams ranked by points scored, updated with Openskill Plackett-Luce on a
  1000 scale (start mu 1000, sigma 333; beta = 25/6 * 40, tau = 25/300 * 40)
  early-season multiplier: new mu = mu + k * (Openskill mu - mu), k = 2, 1.75, 1.5, 1.25 in
  the first rated weeks, then 1
  preseason reversion: mu = 1000 + (1 - 1/3) * (mu - 1000)
  weekly z = (points - week mean) / week sample sd; career z = mean of all weekly z
  background variants: season-to-date mean z, exponentially weighted z (newest week weight
  0.2), a fresh-start rating reset to 1000 each preseason
In plain English:
  A career skill rating per manager ("ELO" on the site): each week is a 12-team race ranked
  by points, and beating managers rated above you moves you up more.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Only the rank matters, not the margin.
  - Reversion anchors on a fixed 1000, not the league mean, so ratings cannot inflate as
    members leave and join. Uncertainty is not reset (resetting widened the spread).
  - A vacant roster's season counts for its last owner, who most likely quit before the
    league year officially ended after playing the season (owner's decision; dynasty 2023
    roster 11 is DeekBurg's).
  - The ratings display career skill; they are not used to forecast games.

16. PLAYER WAR
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/war.py, src/ffapp/espn.py
Data sources:  ESPN start and ownership percentages (data/espn/<season>/season.json and
               week_NN.json, mapped to Sleeper ids through data/espn/crosswalk.json), stats,
               the league's scoring and slots, saved injury statuses. Stored in
               data/war/<league>/<season>.json.
Depends on:    1, 2, 3
Formula:
  consensus lineup (each week): the most-started players with a game fill every position's
    slots (12 teams x slots), then the most-started remaining RB/WR/TE fill the flex
  replacement players: the 3 most-started players with a game past each position's cut
  slot mean m_pos, slot variance v_pos: mean and variance of the consensus starters' actual
    points at each position over the season so far
  opponent ~ N(mu_opp, sd_opp), mu_opp = sum(slots * m_pos), sd_opp = sqrt(sum(slots * v_pos))
  replacement level R_pos = mean of the replacement players' actual points
  weekly win probability = Phi((score - m_pos) / sd_opp), for weeks the player was active
    (a game played for zero points counts as 0; a bye or a week ruled out has no entry)
  expected wins = sum of weekly win probabilities over the active regular-season weeks
  WAR = expected wins - (active weeks * Phi((R_pos - m_pos) / sd_opp))
      = sum over active weeks of [Phi((score - m_pos) / sd_opp) - Phi((R_pos - m_pos) / sd_opp)]
  Cut mode: weekly (every scored week has its own snapshot), yearly (none has: the season
  snapshot ranks every week), merged (some have):
    merged start % = highest percent started in a trusted weekly snapshot (a scored week
    counts only if he played it; the unscored current week always counts); a player not
    seen active in a scored captured week who has an out status (section 3) takes
    max(that, percent owned)
In plain English:
  How many extra wins a player's season was worth compared with a fringe starter at his
  position, in a typical lineup facing a typical opponent. A replacement-level season is
  exactly 0; the best players reach about 2 to 3 wins.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Follows the Stathole Sports method chosen by the owner, with the league's own scoring.
  - Only the opponent varies in the win probability (owner confirmed this is deliberate).
  - WAR accumulates only over weeks the player was active: a bye or a week he was ruled out
    adds nothing, and a game played for zero points counts as zero. (The earlier wording scored
    missed weeks at the replacement level; each such week cancelled exactly, so the values
    are the same, checked for all 6,407 stored player-seasons on 2026-11-19.)
  - The merged cut exists because ESPN's in-season "season" start percentage is only the
    current week's figure (2026 season.json matched week 11 exactly). Ranking every week by
    it treated anyone on a bye or hurt that week as a bench player all season: the 2026 RB
    replacement level came out at 5.0 against 8 to 11 in every completed season. The first
    fix (ownership for everyone not seen active) let backups flood the cut (WR 6.5); the
    chosen rule gives redraft QB 15.6, RB 9.6, WR 8.8, inside the historical range.
  - Why the highest trusted figure: byes and injuries only ever lower a start percentage, so
    the highest trusted figure is closest to a player's role while active. A scored week is
    trusted only if he played it; the unscored current week is always trusted, since a low
    bye or injury figure there cannot win the maximum, and it gives the real figure to a
    player whose team had a bye in the only scored captured week.
  - Why ownership only for injured players: a player hurt for the season before weekly
    capture began (Josh Jacobs in week 11: owned 91.5%, started 8.6%) shows near 0% started
    in every snapshot, while percent owned barely moves during an injured-reserve stint. For
    a healthy backup, ownership far exceeds start rate (owned 50%, started 15%), so it is
    used only with an out status.
  - Past seasons were captured after they ended, so their season snapshot is the genuine
    season-long figure and they keep the yearly cut.
  - From 2027 every week has its own snapshot and the weekly cut applies.

17. POSITIONAL RANKINGS
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/positions.py
Data sources:  Final (past seasons) or current rosters, stats, projections, saved injury
               statuses, Player WAR replacement levels. Built at export time into
               site/data/<league>/positions/<season>.json.
Depends on:    1, 2, 3, 16
Formula:
  PPG = (actual points in final weeks played + projected points in remaining regular-season
         weeks with a projection above 0) / (weeks played + weeks projected)
  a player whose played and projected points total 0 is left out
  axis per league and position (shared by every season):
    low  = min(0, floor5(1st percentile of PPG))
    high = ceil5(max(99th percentile of PPG, highest replacement level, 5)), plus 5 when
           the top value sits exactly on the edge
  rows: managers ordered by their best player's PPG, then the second best, and so on
In plain English:
  Each rostered player is rated by points per game over the regular season, blending what he
  has scored with what he is projected to score, and compared with the Player WAR
  replacement level for his position.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Points from games played for another manager or as a free agent count, because the
    figure rates the player, not the manager's results.
  - The live week counts as projected until it is final, so dots do not jump mid-game.
  - A fixed axis lets seasons be compared by eye; a dynamic axis is the stated fallback if
    outliers make it messy.

18. CALIBRATION
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/calibrate.py
Data sources:  Every completed stored season replayed as of every week through the
               production simulation, with noise pools and drift from the other seasons
               only. Stored in data/calibration/report.json and report.md.
Depends on:    4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10
Formula:
  Score level: PIT = the share of a team's simulated scores below its real score; a flat
    histogram means the noise is the right width. Coverage = how often the real score falls
    inside the simulated 50%, 80% and 95% ranges.
  Game level: favourites binned by forecast win chance (10-point bins), observed win rate
    per bin.
  Season level: reliability bins (forecast chance vs observed rate);
    Brier = mean((forecast - outcome)^2), outcome 1 or 0
    skill = 1 - Brier_model / Brier_baseline
    baseline = a standings reader: each record extrapolated (wins + win% * remaining
    weeks), teams ranked on it (then points), the qualifying positions get 1 and the rest
    0, blended with the outcome's base rate by the share of the season left
  Lineup gap: what the best projected lineup would have added to the started lineup.
  Roster-drift diagnostic: the slope and sd of section 6 by horizon, under frozen and
    floored rosters.
In plain English:
  Past seasons are replayed week by week to check that teams shown at 70% got there about
  70% of the time, and that the forecasts beat simply reading the standings.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Rows after an outcome is decided are left out.
  - Rows within a season are not independent; case counts are shown beside the number of
    independent team-seasons or games.
  - residual_scale may change only when score-level coverage is off across all seasons and
    both leagues, never to make one season's playoff bins look better.

19. MANAGER HISTORY
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/managers.py
Data sources:  Stored matchups (scores, weekly rosters, starters), winners and losers
               brackets, rosters (Sleeper's potential points, FAAB used), users (names,
               team names, logos, avatars), the rivals block in config/leagues.yaml, manager
               ratings, the forecast export, the Games export, positions files and WAR files.
               Exported to site/data/<league>/managers.json.
Depends on:    7, 8, 15, 16, 17
Formula:
  identity: one manager per Sleeper user id across seasons (vacant rosters read as their
    inherited owner); current when holding a roster in the latest season, else retired
  games: every finished regular-season game and winners-bracket playoff game, from both
    sides; weeks after Sleeper's last scored week are left out
  final place: winners bracket placement game p gives place p to its winner and p + 1 to its
    loser; in a toilet bowl Sleeper's "winner" w is the lower scorer who advanced, so
    w gets 13 - p and the loser 12 - p (the p-1 winner is last: the toilet bowl champion);
    in a consolation bracket w gets 6 + p and the loser 7 + p. Without finished brackets
    the regular-season order (wins, then points for) is used
  honours: champion (place 1), runner-up (2), regular-season 1st, most points for,
    playoffs (place <= playoff teams), toilet bowl (last place, toilet-bowl leagues only)
  head to head: per opponent W-L-T, playoff W-L, points for and against, last result
  records: highest and lowest score, biggest win and loss (margin), closest loss, longest
    win and losing streak (within a season), most regular-season points in a season;
    rank = 1 + the number of league entries ever that beat it (ties share a rank; lowest
    score and closest loss rank lowest first)
  all-play: each regular-season week, the score against every other team's that week
  wins above all-play pace = actual wins - (all-play win share * games)
  points against vs league = mean points against per game - league mean per game
  points left on bench = sum over seasons of (Sleeper potential points - actual points)
  rival: the config pair's other roster in the manager's latest season; without a rivals
    block, the nemesis = the opponent with the lowest head-to-head win share over at least
    4 games (ties: the larger points deficit)
  rivalry week record: W-L-T in each season's rivalry week against the rival roster
  form: the last five results of the current season, oldest first
  move windows (from weekly matchup rosters): added = consecutive weeks on the team;
    departed = from leaving until back on the team, or the latest week; each window sums
    the player's weekly WAR, Phi((score - m_pos) / sd) - Phi((R_pos - m_pos) / sd), over its
    active weeks wherever he played; redraft windows end with their season, dynasty windows
    carry over
  best player all-time = the most WAR summed over a player's added windows
  most seasons = seasons with at least one week on the team
  the one that got away = the largest single departed window (shown when above 0); in redraft
    only windows starting in the manager's latest season count (rosters start over each year),
    in dynasty the whole history
In plain English:
  Everything on a manager's page is worked out here once: where they finished each year,
  what they won, how they did against each other manager, their best and worst games
  ranked against the whole league's history, how lucky the schedule was, who their rival
  is, and which players did the most for (or after leaving) their team.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - The toilet bowl champion is the team that "wins" by losing the final: last place. The
    reading of Sleeper's losers bracket was checked against the stored playoff scores.
  - Records include winners-bracket playoff games; luck figures use the regular season only.
  - Rivals follow the roster, so a team keeps its rival when its manager changes; a renamed
    account (SpookyCast, later Jdeguire) is one manager under the latest name.
  - Window WAR uses the same weekly quantity as Player WAR, so windows add up exactly.

20. TEAM NEWS
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/news.py
Data sources:  The games of section 19, the forecast history (data/odds), saved statuses
               and player details (data/injuries, data/players), Sleeper's trending adds
               (data/trending), weekly WAR, the rivals block, and the moves of section 21.
               Exported inside managers.json; the page writes each story from a template
               in site-copy.txt.
Depends on:    3, 9, 16, 19, 21
Formula:
  stories for the current season only (a retired manager's last season):
    result (with the top-scoring starter); rivalry result and preview
    forecast move: |playoff forecast change in a week| >= 10 points
    record: a score above the manager's best before this season, with its league rank
    streak: 3 or more wins or losses in a row
    injury: a rostered player newly given an out status (section 3)
    practice: a rostered starter listed DNP or Limited
    depth chart: a rostered player's depth-chart order changing between weeks
    trending: a rostered player in Sleeper's 10 most-added
    milestone: a player's season WAR on the team passing a whole number
    Team of the Week: the best legal lineup from every rostered player's points that week,
      shown only when it includes one of the manager's players
    final season: for retired managers, their last season and place
    Moves stories (budget B = the season's waiver budget):
      trade: every trade, with what each side got (players, picks, FAAB)
      big bid: a won bid >= 0.25 B, or the season's biggest won bid, with the next bid
      pays off: once, in the week an added move's running WAR (weeks on the team from the
        move) first reaches +1.0
      got away: once, in the week a dropped move's running WAR (weeks away from the move
        until back) first reaches +1.0, naming the team he is on that week
      outbid: a lost bid b with b >= 0.20 B, or (winning bid - b <= 3, a tie included)
        with b >= 0.05 B
  date: games end on the Monday "week" weeks after Labor Day; Thursday for practice,
    injuries, depth charts and previews
  order: newest first; at most 3 player stories and, separately, 3 Moves stories per team
    per week (highest priority kept);
    the feature story is the latest game week's highest priority story: final season >
    record > forecast move of 20 points or more > result
In plain English:
  Short headlines about each manager's season, generated from data the site already has,
  with the most important story of the latest week shown large.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Thresholds keep the feed readable: small forecast moves and too many injury stories
    in one week are left out. Moves thresholds are shares of the budget, because dynasty's
    budget rose from $100 to $150 in 2025; Moves stories have their own cap so a busy
    trade week cannot push out injury news.
  - The text is not generated here: the page writes it from editable templates, so the
    owner can reword any story type in site-copy.txt.

21. MOVES
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/moves.py
Data sources:  Stored Sleeper transactions (data/leagues/<league>/<season>/transactions/),
               the move windows and weekly WAR of section 19, player names from the WAR
               files, stat records and the ESPN crosswalk. Exported inside managers.json.
Depends on:    16, 19
Formula:
  moves: each completed waiver, free-agent or commissioner transaction gives one added or
    dropped row per player for the roster's manager; each trade gives one side per roster
    with the players, picks (season, round) and FAAB (waiver_budget amounts) got and gave;
    players in a trade are never added or dropped rows; drafted players never appear
  claimed by: on a dropped row, the next other manager to add the player while he is away
  move week: Sleeper's leg (offseason moves are filed under week 1 of the next season)
  move WAR: the WAR of the player's matching window (added for an add or a player got,
    departed for a drop or a player given) starting at the first matchup week at or after
    the move and before his next move with this manager
    no games: no week inside that window had a game for him
    pending ("no games yet"): nothing after the move has been scored yet
    picks carry no WAR
  waiver overpay: for a won claim with bid b, let f = the highest failed bid for the same
    player in the same waiver run (same status_updated) with f <= b
    overpay = b - f; with no such f, overpay = b; when f = b the claim is "tied" (won on
    waiver priority) and overpay = 0
  season FAAB: overpaid = the sum of overpay over the season's won claims;
    biggest = the largest winning bid (earliest week on a tie)
In plain English:
  Every add, drop and trade a manager made, what each was worth in WAR for as long as it
  lasted, and how much more than necessary they paid at waivers.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Overpay is hindsight: a bid nobody else made could have been $0, so it counts in full.
  - A failed bid above the winner's lost for another reason (such as roster space), so it
    does not count as the next bid.
  - Move WAR reuses the window sums of section 19, so it agrees with Best Player All-Time
    and the one that got away.
  - Picks get no WAR until trade trees follow each pick to the player drafted with it.

22. GAME CARD STATES
================================================================
Code:          src/ffapp/card_states.py (used by src/ffapp/games_export.py)
Data sources:  The week's matchup rosters (the managers' real starters and bench), the
               stored NFL schedule (each game's state at the last refresh), the live
               lineup rows of the forecast, Sleeper's last scored week, and win
               probability added (section 13). Exported on every card as "state".
Depends on:    7, 9, 13
Formula:
  player game state: none (bye or empty slot), scheduled, in_progress or final, from his
    NFL team's game in the stored schedule; a starter's live row status wins when set
  card state (both teams together):
    final week (Sleeper scored it)              -> final (PW)
    later week                                   -> projected (F1) if the pairing is a
                                                    forecast, else scheduled (F2)
    current week:
      nobody has kicked off                      -> not_started (C)
      a starter is playing                       -> scoring (L1)
      every starter finished (none counts):
        a bench player playing                   -> starters_done_bench_playing (O2)
        a bench player still to play             -> starters_done (O1)
        otherwise                                -> all_done (O3)
      starters still to play, none playing:
        a bench player playing                   -> bench_only (L2)
        some starter has played                  -> paused (P1)
        otherwise                                -> paused_no_points (P2)
  key players for O1, O2, O3 and PW (Actual view): the 4 starters with the largest
    |round(100 * win probability added)|, largest first; ties by position QB, RB, WR, TE,
    DEF, K. The Model view of these states shows the full lineup.
  leader (O1 to O3): the team with more points so far gets the winner check; a tie, none.
In plain English:
  Every game card is in one named state, worked out from who has played, who is playing and
  who is still to play on both rosters, and the page draws each state its own way.
Assumptions and decisions:
  - Real lineups decide, so switching between the Model and Actual views never changes a
    card's state.
  - A player with no game (bye, empty slot) can never score, so he is finished from the
    start; a ruled-out player is finished only when his team's game ends. ESPN reports a
    postponed game as not started (still to play) and a cancelled one as over (0 points).
  - A week becomes past only when Sleeper scores it; until then a finished game is
    unofficial (O1 to O3) though it already shows the winner check.
  - The site refreshes on a schedule, so a card can skip states between refreshes (for
    example from not started straight to paused after Thursday night).
